# Distribution Utility Field Lineman Monsoon Emergency SOP

## Section 1: Waterlogging and Step-Potential Hazards
1. Waterlogging around utility poles creates severe ground potential gradients (Earth Potential Rise). Current spreading radially through water and saturated soil creates dangerous step-potentials between two points on the ground separated by one pace (~1 meter).
2. Prohibited Action: Never take long strides or run in standing water within 10 meters of an energized pole or a pole in leakage alarm.
3. Safe Shuffle Technique: If caught in an energized ground zone or approaching to assess an alarm, keep feet firmly on the ground, touching each other. Shuffle feet slowly forward without lifting either foot from the earth to minimize voltage difference across the body.
4. If leakage current exceeds 30 mA in rain, do not enter the puddle. Maintain a minimum safe distance of 5.0 meters until upstream feeder isolation is confirmed.

## Section 2: Sub-Station Feeder Isolation Request Protocol
1. Prior to any physical contact with a pole structure in alarm, the field lineman must radio or dispatch an isolation permit request to the divisional sub-station.
2. Report the Pole Asset Identifier (e.g. P-001) and observed telemetry (leakage current in mA, touch voltage in V).
3. Wait for verbal authorization and positive confirmation that the upstream circuit breaker has opened and lockout/tagout (LOTO) is in place.

## Section 3: Field De-Energization Verification and Earthing
1. Verification: Use a certified high-voltage non-contact detector stick to confirm that all conductors and the pole chassis are completely de-energized.
2. Discharge: Apply temporary portable earth discharge rods to eliminate trapped capacitive charge before touching pole equipment.
3. Visual Defect Inspection: Inspect for waterlogged cable joints, ruptured insulation sleeves, fallen branches, and cracked ceramic pin insulators.
